Wound Calibration Marker

For AI wound measurement in the WoundProgress Monitor. Plastic Surgery Unit, University of Nigeria Teaching Hospital, Enugu.

Print at exactly 100%. In the print dialog set Scale to 100% and turn off “Fit to page”, “Shrink to fit” and “Scale to fit”. A page printed at 97% makes every wound measure 3% larger, and nothing on screen will indicate that it happened. Verify with a ruler before first use — the check is at the bottom of this sheet.

Marker A — 5 cm bar (standard)

50 mm × 10 mm. Use for most wounds. Cut along the dashed border.

Marker B — 15 cm bar (large wounds)

150 mm × 15 mm. Use when the wound is longer than about 10 cm.

Why this shape and this colour

The app finds the marker by looking for the largest green region in the photograph, then works out which marker it is from the ratio of its length to its width. Both properties matter:

PropertyReason
Green, hue 120° Human skin, blood, slough, granulation tissue and most dressings contain no green of this hue and saturation, so the marker cannot be confused with the wound or the patient.
Aspect ratio 5:1 and 10:1 The app reads 5 cm from a ratio between 3 and 8, and 15 cm from a ratio above 8. These two shapes sit clear of both boundaries, so a slightly oblique photograph cannot tip one into being read as the other.
Solid block, no printed scale The measurement is taken from the block's outline. Printed numbers or tick marks add nothing and can break the shape up under poor lighting.
Matte paper, not glossy Glossy stock produces a specular highlight that reads as white, splitting the green region in two and halving the apparent length.

How to use it — step by step

  1. Prepare the marker. Cut out ONE marker. Laminate it or seal it in a clear specimen bag so it can be wiped between patients. Never place bare paper against an open wound.
  2. Position it in the same plane as the wound. Lay the marker flat on the skin immediately beside the wound, on the same surface and at the same height. A marker resting on the trunk while the wound is on a limb is nearer the camera and will make the wound measure larger than it is. This is the single largest source of error.
  3. Do not let it overlap the wound. Place it 1–2 cm to the side. Keep it clear of the wound edge so the outline of neither is obscured.
  4. Square the camera to the surface. Hold the phone so the lens is directly above the wound and parallel to the skin — not angled. An oblique shot foreshortens the marker, and every measurement derived from it is then too large.
  5. Fill the frame sensibly. Both the wound and the whole marker must be visible, with a little margin. Roughly 20–30 cm from the surface suits most wounds.
  6. Light it evenly. Use indirect daylight or ward lighting. Avoid direct flash, which produces glare on the marker, and avoid photographing in your own shadow.
  7. Remove other green objects from view. Green drapes, green gloves, green gowns and green-labelled dressing packs will compete with the marker; the app uses the largest green region it can find. Move them out of frame.
  8. Take the photograph from the WoundProgress Monitor, using New assessment → Take photo, or select an existing image with Upload from device.
  9. Check the result. If the app reports a calibrated measurement, confirm the figure looks right against what you can see. If it reports that no marker was detected, use Set scale from marker and drag a line along the long edge of the marker, then choose the matching length from the list.
  10. Confirm before saving. The measurements remain editable. The number that enters the record is the one you accept, not the one the app proposed.

Before you photograph — quick check

  • Marker flat on the same surface as the wound
  • Marker beside the wound, not touching or overlapping it
  • Camera square to the skin, not angled
  • Whole marker and whole wound in frame
  • No glare on the marker; no other green in view
  • Marker clean, dry and uncreased

Verify your print

Hold a ruler against Marker A. The green block must measure exactly 50 mm long and 10 mm deep. Marker B must measure 150 mm by 15 mm. If either is out by more than a millimetre the page did not print at full size — reprint with scaling disabled. A marker that is 5% short makes every wound area read about 10% too large, because area scales with the square of the length.

When to replace it

Replace the marker if it becomes creased, stained, faded or curled. A bent marker does not lie flat, and a marker that does not lie flat is not in the plane of the wound. Print a fresh sheet monthly for a busy unit.
